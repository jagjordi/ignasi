This project reads nineteenth- and twentieth-century editorial authority against the evidence of the twentieth-century performing tradition. Comparing **Czerny's, Moscheles's and Kolisch's** metronome markings against a corpus of over one hundred recordings, I document systematic gaps of 11–20% in fast movements and 37–39% in some slow movements.

Strikingly, Kolisch's 1943 markings — derived without corpus evidence — align more closely with recorded practice than either Czerny's or Moscheles's. From this comparison I propose **revised tempo indications as ranges**, grounded in the statistical modal tempi of the corpus {% cite sole2026revised %}.
